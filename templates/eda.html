Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Insights

Comprehensive preliminary evaluation of the Online Retail transactional dataset (541,909 records), highlighting critical data sanitization requirements, outlier dynamics, and commercial purchasing behavior.

Data Quality Issues & Preprocessing

Sanitization Protocol

The raw transactional dataset presented severe data anomalies, high-volume null identifiers, and structural outliers requiring systematic data cleansing before machine learning:

  • Missing Customer IDs: Exactly 135,080 rows (~24.93%) lacked a valid CustomerID. Because customer segmentation requires persistent individual tracking, these rows were filtered out.
  • Missing Descriptions: 1,455 items (0.27%) had null product descriptions and were rectified.
  • Negative Quantities (Returns): Over 10,000 transaction line items recorded negative quantities representing canceled invoices (invoices prefixed with 'C') or damaged inventory reconciliations.
  • Duplicate Invoices: 5,268 exact duplicate rows (0.97% of total records) were detected and purged to eliminate double counting.
  • Extreme Outliers: Massive dispersion was observed in Quantity (-80,995 to +80,995) and UnitPrice (up to £38,970.00 for manual debts), necessitating IQR-based boundary capping.

Commercial & Behavioral Insights

Domain Analysis

Statistical evaluation revealed distinct purchasing patterns, temporal seasonality, and geographic concentration across the retail ecosystem:

  • Geographic Dominance: Approximately 85% to 89% of all transaction volume and revenue originated from the United Kingdom, followed by Germany, France, and EIRE.
  • Strong Q4 Seasonality: Order volume surges significantly in November and December, representing holiday gift preparation, year-end stock accumulation, and wholesale restocking.
  • Operational Peak Windows: Order activity is concentrated on Tuesday and Wednesday, peaking between 8:00 AM and 3:00 PM GMT during standard European business hours.
  • Right-Skewed Distributions: Recency, Frequency, and Monetary metrics exhibit heavy positive skewness, where the top 10% of customers generate more than 60% of total commercial revenue.
  • Catalog Diversity: High affinity observed in household gifts, decorative homeware, kitchen utensils, and party accessories.
